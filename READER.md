# The reader's memory

This is a record of what a reader knows, suspects, and is waiting for after reading only the fourteen entries of Felix's notebook so far, in order, once. It is not a summary of the story; it is a log of a reader's state of mind as it changed entry by entry. Nothing here comes from outside the pages.

## After entry 1 — 2026-09-20 — Bath, dans la cuisine

**What I now know**
- The writer is in Bath, in his mother's kitchen, at 7 a.m., in rain. His mother puts coffee in front of him without speaking. He believes she already knows something he hasn't said aloud.

**What I suspect**
- He is about to announce a departure or a decision, and it's one his mother has been expecting for a while. High confidence: the whole entry is a held breath.
- "Il pleut, évidemment" — the weariness in that *évidemment* suggests England is not where he wants to be. Medium.
- The writer writes in French in an English town; the family is at least partly French. Medium.

**What he's not saying**
- The thing itself. The entry is three sentences long and ends exactly at the moment of not saying.

**Open questions**
- What is the decision? Why Bath? Who else is in this house?

## After entry 2 — 2026-09-26 — La décision

**What I now know**
- The decision: return to Chamonix "pour de bon" and prepare the guide entrance exam (*probatoire*). He's been circling it for three years, since someone named Eric said "Tu devrais faire ça pour de vrai" at the edge of a piste, taking off skins — so Eric is a ski-touring companion, somewhere outside Europe ("Je suis parti en Europe" afterward).
- Family: a father who worries through practical questions (money, insurance); a mother born in Chamonix, who says only the word "Chamonix"; a Chloé on the phone who says he decided this three years ago. He plans to get a studio, call Bertrand (an old guide who climbed with his grandfather), and fill gaps in his climbing list: ice, mixed, serious descents. One winter to do it. Savings plus ski-shop shifts in December.
- Train Thursday: Bath–London–Paris–Saint-Gervais–the little red train.

**What I suspect**
- Chloé is his sister (tone, the phone call folded into the family reactions). High.
- He keeps stones from mountains ("dans le sac, avec les cailloux"). High.
- "Cette fois, je ne repartirai pas" — a man telling himself something. The insistence on *this time* means there was a last time he left, and the sentence is aimed at his own doubt as much as at the reader. Medium-high.
- The grandfather is spoken of in the imperfect ("grimpait"); probably dead. Medium.
- The mother's line — "elle sait ce que ça coûte et ce que ça donne" — carries weight beyond cost of living. Something in this family has been paid to the mountains. Low-medium.

**What he's not saying**
- Where he has been for three years and what he was doing there; what "un été entier de routes et de sommets" was and with whom. No partner, no relationship mentioned at all — conspicuous for a man reorganising his life.
- Why Eric's remark went unanswered for three years.

**Open questions**
- Where is Eric? What did Felix leave, and why does he say he won't leave again? What happened to the grandfather?

## After entry 3 — 2026-10-02 — Argentière, quatorze mètres carrés

**What I now know**
- He has a 14 m² studio in Argentière facing the Aiguille Verte and the Drus. The landlady, Josiane, 72, recognises him as "le petit-fils de Marcel. Tu as ses mains." Grandfather = Marcel, a known figure in the valley.
- He runs to Lognan; the Grands Montets are closed until December. He has weekend shop work in Chamonix from December. His mother sends Bertrand's number with one word: "Vas-y."

**What I suspect**
- Marcel was a guide, or the local equivalent, and is dead — Josiane speaks of him as someone recognised in a grandson's hands, not someone you could go and visit. Medium-high.
- The mother's "Vas-y" is not neutral encouragement; it reads like someone releasing him toward something she once left. Medium.
- "La vallée se prépare, comme moi" — he is happiest when the world is empty and expectant. Note it.

**What he's not saying**
- Nothing evasive here; the entry is genuinely happy. But the studio is exactly the size for one person, and he doesn't remark on it.

**Open questions**
- What will Bertrand see in him? Who is Marcel, really?

## After entry 4 — 2026-10-06 — Bertrand

**What I now know**
- Bertrand: Les Praz, 63, guide since 1988, climbed with Marcel. Reads the list in silence; verdict: rock fine, ski fine, "La glace, il n'y a rien." He prescribes gullies, mixed, two or three serious ice routes, descents Felix wouldn't choose. "On verra." He corrects "conquérir" with one eyebrow. Proposes a ridge in the Aiguilles Rouges Thursday, "pour voir comment tu marches."
- First mention of **Canada**: "J'ai appris ça au Canada, avec Eric." So Eric is in Canada; that's where the three years went.

**What I suspect**
- Bertrand is assessing character, not stride — Felix says so himself, and I think he's right. Bertrand looked at hands, boots, then him, in that order.
- Bertrand said one thing about Marcel "comme on constate la météo." Both he and Felix handle the grandfather with the same reticence. There's a story there nobody is telling. Medium.
- "Ce sont des hommes dont la modestie n'est pas une politesse : c'est une méthode" — Felix admires this and doesn't have it. He talks; his admiration for silence is the admiration of someone outside it. Medium.

**What he's not saying**
- Whether Marcel is alive. Whether he ever climbed with him. Nothing about what "Canada" was beyond a lesson in etiquette.

**Open questions**
- What was Felix doing in Canada? Will Bertrand take him on?

## After entry 5 — 2026-10-11 — Revelstoke, première neige

**What I now know**
- Eric sends a photo of Mount Mackenzie from Revelstoke, asks where he's skiing this winter. Felix first went up there December 2023, taught by Eric (avalanche course, Québec French). He skinned with his grandfather from age twelve. He was a ski instructor at Revelstoke.
- **Addie**: a client who photographed snow on cedar branches rather than summits. He never had her number: "elle était ma cliente, j'étais son moniteur, et il y avait une ligne que je n'ai pas voulu franchir." On the last day, end of March, he told her he didn't know if he'd come back. She looked "un peu déçue." "J'aurais dû dire autre chose… je ne sais toujours pas si j'ai eu raison."
- He answers Eric: "Ici. Chez moi. Je fais la liste."

**What I suspect**
- This is the unsaid thing from entry 1, or a large part of it. The entry is titled *Revelstoke* and ends with "chez moi," and the two don't reconcile. High.
- Addie is introduced sideways — via cedars in a photo — and then occupies the longest paragraph. The casualness is structural deflection. High.
- "Une ligne que je n'ai pas voulu franchir" — the line is being credited with a decision that was really his fear. He half-admits this: "je ne sais toujours pas si j'ai eu raison." Medium-high.
- "Elle ment mieux" (of Canadian snow) — the writer is preoccupied with things that lie well. Note it.

**What he's not saying**
- How many winters he taught there; whether Addie came more than once; what "autre chose" he should have said. He never says her full name, where she's from, or anything that would let a reader (or him) find her.

**Open questions**
- Who is Addie? Was March her only visit? Is "chez moi" true?

## After entry 6 — 2026-10-14 — Les Crochues, en guide

**What I now know**
- Tuesday, Col des Montets at 6 a.m., walk-in via Lac Blanc. Bertrand hands him the rope: "Aujourd'hui, c'est toi le guide," and plays a realistic client. Felix guides the traverse badly: rope late once ("ton client est déjà par terre"), too fast in the downclimb. Verdict at the frozen lake: "Tu grimpes bien. Tu ne guides pas encore. Tu grimpes pour toi ; un guide grimpe pour celui qui est derrière." Red stone in pocket.

**What I suspect**
- "Tu grimpes pour toi" is the diagnosis of the whole notebook, not just the day. He decides alone, leaves alone, and calls it clarity. Bertrand has named it in six words. High.
- Bertrand is measuring him against Marcel without saying so. Medium.

**What he's not saying**
- Nothing much; this is the most honest entry so far. The one small thing: "Ça compte" — he needs to tell himself it counts.

**Open questions**
- Can he learn to climb for the one behind? Who, in his life, is "behind"?

## After entry 7 — 2026-10-19 — Une page que je referme

**What I now know**
- "Sur la liste des choses que je ne dis à personne": he periodically looks up the dates of the Canadian guide training Eric did. "Pas pour l'année prochaine. Peut-être dans quelques années. Peut-être jamais." He closes the page before finishing.

**What I suspect**
- "Cette fois, je ne repartirai pas" (entry 2) was overstated, and he knows it. High.
- There is a *list* of things he tells no one, and this is one item. Addie is another. High.
- A Canadian guide qualification means guiding in Canada — near Revelstoke — in March. The training and the woman are the same wish wearing two coats. Medium-high.
- The entry's brevity is the avoidance itself; he closes the entry the way he closes the page.

**What he's not saying**
- Anything at all beyond the confession. He doesn't name what the training would be *for*.

**Open questions**
- Will he tell Bertrand? Eric? Is the Chamonix exam a step toward Canada or a substitute for it?

## After entry 8 — 2026-10-27 — Vingt centimètres

**What I now know**
- First real snow. Josiane says it won't stay. Gear day: two second-hand axes from a friend of Bertrand's whose "genoux ont décidé pour lui." Money lasts until March "si je ne casse rien et si je ne tombe pas malade." Théo phones: arriving Saturday for Toussaint, "sans le vélo, pour une fois."

**What I suspect**
- After the confession of entry 7, this entry is deliberately, comfortingly practical — crampons, glue, arithmetic. He steadies himself with tasks. Medium-high.
- The finances are genuinely one accident away from failure; he says so lightly. That fragility will matter. Medium.
- Théo is an old friend and a cyclist; "pour une fois" means the bike is usually there. High.
- A chamois, alone, having the same idea as him — the writer keeps noticing solitary things.

**What he's not saying**
- Canada, entirely. Not one word after last week's page.

**Open questions**
- Who is Théo to him? Will the money hold?

## After entry 9 — 2026-11-01 — Théo

**What I now know**
- Théo comes from Lyon; friends since age twelve — so Felix grew up in or around Lyon. "C'est moi qui trouve le chemin et lui qui trouve les gens." August 2024: Théo flew to Vancouver with his bike and they rode to San Francisco in three weeks — rain in Oregon, punctures in the redwoods, wrong turn at Astoria, Golden Gate in fog. "Le seul voyage de ma vie où je n'ai rien décidé."
- At the Col des Montets, Théo asks "Et le Canada, c'est fini ?" — "Je ne sais pas." — "Tu dis ça pour tout, en ce moment." Théo leaves an inner tube; Felix has no bike here.

**What I suspect**
- "On a parlé du Canada, forcément. Pas de Revelstoke : du vélo." He steered even his oldest friend away from Revelstoke. Théo has not been told about Addie. High.
- The trip he loves most is the one where he decided nothing — for a man who "finds the way," that's a confession about the cost of always deciding. Medium-high.
- The inner tube is a promise of return, left on the table without explanation, and he keeps it. He'll ride again with Théo. Medium.
- August 2024 sits between his first and second Revelstoke winters. San Francisco as destination — I file it, no more. Low.

**What he's not saying**
- Why "je ne sais pas" has become his answer to everything. Théo names it; Felix agrees and moves on.

**Open questions**
- What does Théo suspect? Why was the destination San Francisco?

## After entry 10 — 2026-11-09 — Le plan pour l'hiver

**What I now know**
- Bertrand writes the winter: December ice (la Crèmerie, Cogne); January Vallée Blanche descents; February longer ski routes and gullies; March a multi-day tour, Chamonix–Zermatt, "s'il y a de la neige et un partenaire"; then the exam. Three rules: never alone on ice; a record card after every route; count days outside, not routes. Shop starts 5 December; studio paid to March.
- "Et après ? … Peut-être le Canada, un jour." Eric would say yes; Felix won't ask him "cette année."
- Copying the sheet, he stops at "mars": "c'est le mois où elle venait à Revelstoke, chaque année. Si je ne suis pas là-bas en mars, je ne saurai pas si elle est revenue." He writes "mars" and moves to April.

**What I suspect**
- "Elle" — no name. He can't write "Addie" in this entry. He could in entry 5, when she was decor. Now she's a date on a calendar. High.
- "Chaque année" — she came more than one winter. She's a returning client over years, not a single-season acquaintance. This quietly enlarges everything in entry 5. High.
- "Pour une fois, ça ne me dérange pas" (about not knowing the future) is written two paragraphs before the March paragraph shows that it does. Too reasonable. High.
- Bertrand's rule "jamais seul" is about ice; the reader hears it about everything else.
- The March collision is now set: Chamonix–Zermatt vs. Revelstoke in March. The notebook is heading there. Medium-high.

**What he's not saying**
- Her name. Whether he could find out if she came back (he could ask Eric — he doesn't say so).

**Open questions**
- Who will be the March partner? Will he ask Eric anything at all?

## After entry 11 — 2026-12-03 — Cogne, première glace

**What I now know**
- Cogne with Bertrand, −12°C. First lead on ice; a badly placed second screw — "Celle-là, elle tenait ton chapeau. Pas toi." Rewarming hands, ten minutes of pain side by side. Afternoon: "La glace, c'est les pieds, pas les bras."
- In the car, Bertrand on Marcel, three minutes: the Couturier couloir on the Aiguille Verte, 1986, Marcel in front, not ten words all day, at the top "Bon, on descend." Then: "Tu as ses mains. Tu n'as pas encore son silence."
- First record card written. "Ce qui n'a pas marché : … l'idée qu'on ne se fait avoir que sur les cascades difficiles."

**What I suspect**
- The Aiguille Verte is the mountain in his window. Marcel's greatest day is what he looks at every morning. Medium-high that this is not incidental.
- "Pas encore son silence" — Bertrand means it as a standard; I'm not sure the notebook agrees with him. Felix's failure with Addie was a failure of silence — he said too little, or (entry 5) the wrong thing. Silence may be the family disease as much as the family virtue. Medium.
- "On ne se fait avoir que sur les cascades difficiles" is the truest sentence about his life so far: it's the easy route — a client, a lesson, small talk about cedars — that caught him.
- "Je crois qu'on se connaît mieux comme ça qu'en parlant" — he's constructing a philosophy that excuses not speaking. Medium.

**What he's not saying**
- Whether Marcel's silence cost anyone anything. Still nothing on how Marcel died, if he did.

**Open questions**
- Is the grandfather's silence the model or the warning?

## After entry 12 — 2026-12-05 — Premier samedi au magasin

**What I now know**
- Eight hours fitting boots. A Lyon family; he recognises the accent (confirms Lyon as home). A customer asks if he's a guide: "Pas encore" — first time to a stranger.

**What I suspect**
- "Pas encore" is a promise he's made to himself in public now. Small, real. He's also, for the first time, the one being served questions by a client — the instructor's role reversed into shop work. Low-medium significance.

**What he's not saying**
- Nothing; a tired man's entry. The card "déjà dans le sac" shows Bertrand's rules taking hold.

**Open questions**
- None new.

## After entry 13 — 2026-12-13 — Premières traces

**What I now know**
- Grands Montets open; he skis piste, refuses off-piste on a thin snowpack: "Ce n'est pas de la prudence, c'est de la lecture." Crèmerie last Sunday not yet holding. Bertrand corrects the first card: too long ("une fiche, ça ne raconte pas, ça note"), missing return time.
- Chloé calls from Edinburgh (exams — a student); she announces she's coming 21 December for a week, will sleep on the floor; the parents come three days, hotel in Les Houches, "le village de maman." Dad, via Chloé, asks whether the insurance covers ice. Chloé is confirmed as sister ("les parents").
- "Content ?" — "Je crois." "Content, ici, ça veut dire fatigué, au clair, et dans la bonne vallée." He'll take Chloé up to Lognan on skins.

**What I suspect**
- "Je crois" — Théo's diagnosis holding. And his definition of content has no person in it. High.
- "Dans la bonne vallée" — said against another valley. He's still measuring. Medium-high.
- The family is closer and warmer than his terse reporting lets on; Chloé doesn't ask, she announces, and he's counting the days. He's less alone than he writes himself. Medium-high.
- Bertrand's "ça ne raconte pas, ça note" is a rule the notebook itself constantly breaks. The cards are for Bertrand; the notebook is where the story leaks.

**What he's not saying**
- Whether he'll tell Chloé — the direct one — about Canada or Addie. She's the person most likely to ask.

**Open questions**
- What will Chloé see and say?

## After entry 14 — 2026-12-19 — Bay Area

**What I now know**
- A couple from San Francisco at the shop; the woman says "the Bay Area." That evening the rest comes up.
- Addie came last season twice, January and March; she'd already come two winters before that — three winters in all, matching his three. He wondered from January whether she came back for the snow "ou pour autre chose." He never dared ask. She was methodical, unafraid of steep slopes but needing to understand them; had seen more countries than him; "fière de ses racines et attachée à ses années en Amérique du Nord." She listened to everything: Lyon, the valley, his grandfather, the bike ride to San Francisco, "sa côte."
- "J'ai peut-être franchi la ligne sans rien dire." "Je me dis que ce n'était pas convenable, à l'époque. Je ne suis pas sûr que ce soit la vraie raison."
- First time written: going back to Canada "ce n'est pas seulement pour la formation ; c'est aussi l'idée que je pourrais la revoir là-bas." No number, no email, no employer name. "Une femme dont je ne connais pas l'adresse, et dont je connais le prénom chinois — Anyu."
- Tomorrow ice; Chloé on the 21st. "La question reste ouverte."

**What I suspect**
- Entry 5's story is being revised. Then: a line he chose not to cross, one last day. Now: the line was crossed in the talking, the reason was not propriety, and it wasn't one winter but three. The notebook has caught its writer editing his own past. High.
- "Sa côte": she's from the Bay Area. He and Théo rode to San Francisco in August 2024, after his first winter with her as a client. I now doubt that destination was innocent, whether or not he knew it. Medium.
- She told him her Chinese name. Clients don't do that; people do. That single word — Anyu — is the most intimate fact in the notebook and he places it last, after listing everything he doesn't know, as if it were a footnote. It isn't. High.
- "Attachée à ses années en Amérique du Nord" — the past tense hides something: perhaps she's not from North America originally, perhaps she no longer lives there. He knows, and doesn't say. Medium.
- "On peut garder quelqu'un des années sans le moindre moyen de le joindre" — this is the sentence that sounds too reasonable. He was her instructor. The ski school has her booking details. Eric is *right there*. He has at least one obvious way to reach her and he narrates having none. High.
- The proportions matter: "Le couple est reparti. Demain, la glace ; Chloé arrive le 21." — three flat sentences slammed on top of the confession, then "la question reste ouverte," which is the closest he comes to saying he wants her.

**What he's not saying**
- That he could ask Eric. That the Chamonix–Zermatt raid in March and Revelstoke in March are the same week. That "la question" has a name and he's just written it.

**Open questions**
- Will he say any of this to Chloé, who will ask? Will he write to Eric? What does the exam become if the real destination is a person? And the grandfather — the family's mountain "cost" — is still unexplained, and I no longer think it's going to stay that way.

## Where I stand

I think this notebook is about a man who mistakes his own reticence for a method he inherited, and who is slowly finding out that the inheritance — Marcel's hands, Marcel's silence — includes the thing that keeps failing him. The training log, the cards, Bertrand's rules are real and I believe he'll do the work; but the exam is the frame, not the picture. What he wants and hasn't admitted in a full sentence is to be in Revelstoke in March, to ask Anyu the question he didn't ask in January, and to find out whether she came back for the snow — and he'd like a Canadian guide's ticket to make that look like a career decision rather than a personal one. I expect Chloé to force something open over Christmas, Eric to be asked (or to volunteer) what Felix pretends is unaskable, and March to arrive as a choice between a rope to Zermatt and a flight. I also expect the grandfather's story to turn out to be about what silence cost, not what it earned. I could be wrong about the flight; I don't think I'm wrong that "la question reste ouverte" is the least closed sentence he's written.
