# The reader's memory

> **About the story, not about you.** What an imaginary first-time reader of Felix's notebook knows, suspects
> and is waiting for, rebuilt from the entries alone by `tools/reader.sh`; used to plan the plot. For *your*
> French (what you look up, click and forget), see `LEARNER-PROFILE.md`.

This is what one reader holds in mind after reading Felix's notebook once, in order, up to the entry of 19 December 2026. It uses only the fourteen entries. It separates what the text has established, what a careful reader infers, and where the writer seems to look away. Each section records only what changed after that entry. (The notebook never names him. "Felix" comes from the framing, not the text.)

## After entry 1 — 2026-09-20 — Bath, dans la cuisine

**What I now know**
- He is in Bath, at seven in the morning, in his mother's kitchen. It is raining.
- He has something to say that he hasn't said yet. His mother already seems to know what it is.

**What I suspect**
- He's an adult visiting his parents rather than living with them. The kitchen scene feels like a return. Low–moderate confidence.
- He writes in French, so he is probably French or bilingual, and the family is settled in England.

**What he's not saying**
- What the thing is. The whole entry is built around an unspoken announcement.

**Open questions**
- What is the announcement? How does his mother know?

## After entry 2 — 2026-09-26 — La décision

**What I now know**
- The announcement: he is moving back to Chamonix "pour de bon" to prepare the mountain-guide entrance exam (the *probatoire*, run by the ENSA). He has one winter.
- Eric, whom he knew somewhere with skins and pistes, told him three years ago: « Tu devrais faire ça pour de vrai. » He never answered. Then he spent a summer climbing in Europe.
- His father worries through practical questions (money, insurance). Asking about insurance means he approves.
- His mother was born in Chamonix. Her only comment was « Chamonix. »
- Chloé, reached by phone, says he decided this three years ago and has only just noticed.
- Bertrand is an old guide who climbed with his grandfather. What his list lacks: ice, mixed, serious descents.
- He takes the train on Thursday, and says « Cette fois, je ne repartirai pas. »

**What I suspect**
- Chloé is a sister. Fairly confident, from the tone and the way the family is grouped.
- "Je suis parti en Europe" is how someone talks when he was based outside Europe. Eric and the pistes were probably in North America.
- There was an earlier departure from Chamonix. "Cette fois" and "je rentre" point to one.
- The mother "sait ce que ça coûte". That hints the mountains have cost this family something, possibly the grandfather. Low confidence, but I'm noting it.

**What he's not saying**
- Why it took three years. He accepts Chloé's version (« elle a probablement raison ») a little too quickly, as if agreeing were a way to end the conversation.
- « Je ne repartirai pas » is a vow, and vows are usually made against a temptation. Leave for where?
- Entry 1 was six days before "hier soir, je l'ai dit à table". He carried the unsaid thing for almost a week.

**Open questions**
- Where was he for those three years? Who is Eric? Who was the grandfather, and is he alive?

**What I now know**

- His grandfather's name is Marcel, and he is known in the valley.

- His mother is quietly backing this more than the father is.

**What he's not saying**
- Nothing clear yet. This entry is openly happy, and "un silence qui attend" reads as a description of himself.

**Open questions**
- What will Bertrand make of him?

## After entry 4 — 2026-10-06 — Bertrand

**What I now know**
- Bertrand is 63, lives in Les Praz, and has been with the Compagnie since 1988.
- His verdict: « Le rocher, ça va. Le ski, ça va. La glace, il n'y a rien. » The list is probably not enough for March, but not impossible. « On verra. »
- Felix learned "in Canada, with Eric" not to comment while someone reads his list. So Eric = Canada, confirmed.
- Felix used the word "conquérir", and Bertrand's raised eyebrow corrected him.
- Bertrand plans a ridge in the Aiguilles Rouges, "pour voir comment tu marches".

**What I suspect**
- Bertrand says little about Marcel on purpose. Saying it "comme on constate la météo" suggests there is more to say, and that it is weighty.
- Felix likes being judged by laconic men. It lets him hand the judging over to them.

**What he's not saying**
- Little yet. His interest in Bertrand's silence is noticeable, though.

**Open questions**
- What will Bertrand really be looking at on the ridge?

## After entry 5 — 2026-10-11 — Revelstoke, première neige

**What I now know**
- Revelstoke, British Columbia. He first went in December 2023.
- Eric taught him the avalanche course at Rogers Pass and speaks Québécois French. Eric's line: « La montagne, elle te dit non. Toi, tu l'écoutes. »
- Felix was a ski instructor there.
- **Addie.** She was his client. She stopped mid-slope for the sea of clouds over the Columbia valley.
- He never had her number: "il y avait une ligne que je n'ai pas voulu franchir". On the last day, in late March, he told her he didn't know if he'd return. She looked a little disappointed and asked nothing. « J'aurais dû dire autre chose. »
- Eric's reply to him ends with « est-ce que je mangeais assez ? »

**What I suspect**
- This is the reason for the vow in entry 2. The temptation to leave again is Revelstoke, and inside Revelstoke is Addie. Fairly confident.
- Eric's photo triggered Addie, not snow. He goes straight from the ridge line to "la vue qu'Addie aimait le plus."
- The question about eating tells me Eric has seen him in a bad way before, or hears something now. It may be the money, or something heavier. Low–moderate confidence. I'm watching for it.
- Eric's « Tu skies où c't'hiver ? » may be a real question about whether Felix is coming back.

**What he's not saying**
- The ethics of the "ligne" are offered as the reason. It sounds reasonable. It may be true. It may also be where he hides.
- « Je ne sais toujours pas si j'ai eu raison » is the first crack in the tidy decision of entry 2.
- He runs off after putting the phone down. Movement is his way of not staying with a feeling.

**Open questions**
- How many seasons did he know Addie? Was it only one? Does Eric know about her?

## After entry 6 — 2026-10-14 — Les Crochues, en guide

**What I now know**
- Bertrand made him guide the Crochues traverse while Bertrand played the client.
- Verdict: « Tu grimpes bien. Tu ne guides pas encore. Tu grimpes pour toi ; un guide grimpe pour celui qui est derrière. »
- He keeps a red stone from the route.
- The radio reports rockfall on the Goûter and huts closed in August.

**What I suspect**
- Bertrand's diagnosis is aimed at more than rope work. Felix accepts it at once as "juste", and it fits what he has shown so far: someone who acts alone and doesn't turn around in time. "Mal au cou à force de regarder derrière" is literal here. It will read differently later.

**What he's not saying**
- Nothing evasive. This is the most honest, least guarded entry so far.

**Open questions**
- Will he learn it "dans les jambes"?

## After entry 9 — 2026-11-01 — Théo

**What I now know**
- Théo is a friend since age twelve, from Lyon. He fixes things and finds people. Felix finds the way.
- In August 2024 they cycled from Vancouver to San Francisco.
- Théo asked « Et le Canada, c'est fini ? » Felix said he didn't know. Théo: « Tu dis ça pour tout, en ce moment. »
- Théo left an inner tube, and Felix, who has no bike here, keeps it.

**What I suspect**
- Felix grew up at least partly in Lyon. That sits oddly with parents in Bath and a mother from the Chamonix valley. The family moved, and I don't know when.
- "Pas de Revelstoke : du vélo" is deliberate steering. He talks about Canada while avoiding the part that matters.
- The inner tube is Théo's joke or gift, implying "you'll need a bike again." Felix keeping it is a small admission.

**What he's not saying**
- He didn't tell Théo, the one person with "des droits", about Addie, or at least doesn't record doing so.
- « Le seul voyage où je n'ai rien décidé » — he values not having to decide. Everything else in the notebook is about a decision he can't make.

**Open questions**
- Does Théo know about Addie?

## After entry 10 — 2026-11-09 — Le plan pour l'hiver

**What I now know**
- Bertrand's plan: December ice (the Crèmerie, Cogne), January skiing, February long tours and gullies, March a Chamonix–Zermatt ski tour, then the spring exam.
- Three rules: never alone on ice, a record card after every route, count days outside and not routes.
- The shop starts 5 December. The studio is paid to March.
- He writes: « Mars, c'est le mois où elle venait à Revelstoke, chaque année. » Then « je suis passé à avril. »

**What I suspect**
- "Elle" needs no name. That is the tell: she's the default "she" in his head. She came every year, so she was a regular across several seasons, not a one-off. That changes entry 5's picture.
- March is the collision point. The Chamonix–Zermatt tour and her March week at Revelstoke fall in the same month. Confident he feels this.

**What he's not saying**
- « Pour une fois, ça ne me dérange pas » about not knowing his future. That is too calm, and it comes one paragraph before he stops dead on the word "mars".
- He says Eric "won't say it, because I won't ask him this year." He presents it as restraint. It is also a way of not finding out.
- "Je ne le saurais pas de toute façon" is fatalism made to sound like logic.

**Open questions**
- How many years did she come? What will he do with March?

## After entry 11 — 2026-12-03 — Cogne, première glace

**What I now know**
- He did his first real ice at Cogne with Bertrand. He placed a bad second screw: « Celle-là, elle tenait ton chapeau. Pas toi. »
- The thaw pain in both men's hands.
- Bertrand and Marcel climbed the Couturier couloir in 1986. Marcel said barely ten words and at the top: « Bon, on descend. »
- « Tu as ses mains. Tu n'as pas encore son silence. » Felix doesn't answer, "ce qui était la réponse."
- He wrote his first record card.

**What I suspect**
- The family admires silence, and he is trying to earn it. But his own history is full of silences that were failures: he never answered Eric, and he said nothing to Addie. The notebook is where he isn't silent. Moderate confidence that this tension is central.
- Marcel is dead. Still not stated, still almost certain.

**What he's not saying**
- Whether Marcel died in the mountains. Twice now the grandfather comes up without a death. The mother's « ce que ça coûte » still hangs there.

**Open questions**
- What happened to Marcel?

## After entry 13 — 2026-12-13 — Premières traces

**What I now know**
- The Grands Montets opened. He refused the off-piste: « ce n'est pas de la prudence, c'est de la lecture. »
- Bertrand corrected his first card: « une fiche, ça ne raconte pas, ça note. »
- Chloé is a student in Edinburgh and is coming on 21 December for a week. Their parents are staying three days in Les Houches, "le village de maman". Chloé is now clearly his sister.

**What I suspect**
- Les Houches versus "born in Chamonix" is a loose use of "Chamonix" for the valley. Not a contradiction.
- Chloé's visit is where the notebook will break open. She's direct, sees through him, and will sleep on his floor for a week.
- « Une fiche, ça ne raconte pas, ça note. » The notebook is the opposite of a card. It tells stories, and that's where he keeps what the cards can't hold.

**What he's not saying**
- « Je crois » when asked if he's content. His definition, "fatigué, au clair, et dans la bonne vallée", is careful. "Dans la bonne vallée" implies there's another valley.

**Open questions**
- Will he tell Chloé?

## After entry 14 — 2026-12-19 — Bay Area

**What I now know**
- A San Francisco customer said "the Bay Area", and that night everything came back.
- "Last season she came twice, January and March." She had "already come two winters" before that. So he knew her for three seasons.
- She was lively and funny, wanted to understand steep slopes, and had skied Tahoe, Jackson Hole, Alta and Mammoth. She was proud of her roots and attached to her years in North America.
- The Bay Area is "sa côte". He told her about Lyon, his grandfather, and the bike ride to San Francisco.
- He now writes that he may have crossed the line « sans rien dire, en racontant des choses qu'on ne dit pas à une cliente. »
- He admits, "pour la première fois", that going back to Canada is also about seeing her.
- Her Chinese name is **Anyu**. Addie, presumably, is her English name.
- He has no number, no email, no employer.

**What I suspect**
- The feeling was mutual, or at least plausibly so. A client who rebooks three winters running, adds a second trip, and looks disappointed at "I don't know if I'll be back" is a signal. He saw it: « si elle revenait pour la neige ou pour autre chose. » Moderate–high confidence that he believes she did.
- That she told him her Chinese name says the conversations were personal on both sides.
- Entry 5 was a cleaned-up version. "Une ligne que je n'ai pas voulu franchir" has become "j'ai peut-être franchi la ligne." He is letting the story get truer as he goes.
- The "real reason" he didn't ask, the one he isn't sure of, is fear. He was afraid of the answer, or afraid of committing to come back. The honourable reason covered both. Moderately confident.

**What he's not saying**
- The entry slides from the customer ("elle a dit « the Bay Area »") to Anyu ("la saison dernière, elle est venue") with no name and no transition. The reader has to supply her.
- He writes « La seule chose dont je suis sûr, c'est que j'ai peut-être… ». That is certainty about a maybe, a sentence that won't commit even while confessing.
- He lists every way he *can't* reach her and none of the ways he could:
  - The ski school has her bookings.
  - Eric is still in Revelstoke and writes to him regularly.
  - He has a name, a region, and her habit of coming in March.
- "On peut garder quelqu'un des années sans le moindre moyen de le joindre" sounds wistful. It is also a choice being dressed up as impossibility.
- « Je n'ai jamais osé » appears here for the first time. It is the word he has been avoiding since entry 5.

**Open questions**
- Will he ask Eric? Does Eric already know?
- Did she come back last March for him, and did she know he might not return?
- Will Chloé get it out of him?
- What happens in March?

## Where I stand

On the surface this is a notebook about earning the right to be a guide. Underneath, it's about a man who learned from his family that silence is a virtue and is finding out that his own silences cost him. He never answered Eric, and he let Anyu walk off with "je ne sais pas." Bertrand's line fits more than climbing: he climbs for himself and hasn't yet learned to turn round for the one behind him. What he wants and hasn't admitted is not the ACMG course. He wants to find her, and he could start tomorrow by writing to Eric or the ski school. The notebook keeps listing reasons he can't in place of that one easy move. I expect Chloé's week to force the question into words. I expect March to become the real test, Zermatt against Revelstoke. I'd bet he stays for the exam but finally asks Eric, and that the vow "je ne repartirai pas" won't survive the notebook in its current form. I may be wrong about her. The text so far has only given me his side.
