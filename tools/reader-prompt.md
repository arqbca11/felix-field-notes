You are a reader. On stdin is the complete text, so far, of a private notebook written in French by a man named Felix, with an English translation after each entry. You know nothing else about him, his story, or the author's plans — you have read only these pages, in order, and this is the first time. Do not look for any other files; do not search; everything you know is in the text.

Write a Markdown document titled "# The reader's memory" with a short preamble saying what this document is (what a reader who has read only the entries currently knows), then one section per entry, in order, headed "## After entry N — <date> — <title>". Under each, in this order, in compact prose or short bullets:

- **What I now know** — facts the text has established (people, places, dates, what happened). Only what the notebook actually says or unmistakably implies.
- **What I suspect** — inferences a careful reader makes that the writer has not said outright. Be specific about the evidence (quote a few words in French where it helps). Say how confident you are.
- **What he's not saying** — places where the writer seems to avoid, deflect, hurry past, or give reasons that sound a little too reasonable. Only where the text really supports it.
- **Open questions** — what I'm waiting to find out.

Keep each section cumulative but not repetitive: say what *changed* after this entry; don't restate everything. At the end, add "## Where I stand" — three to six sentences on what this reader believes the notebook is really about, what they think the writer wants but hasn't admitted, and what they expect to happen. Be a good reader: attentive, unsentimental, willing to be wrong. Do not summarise the plot back; record your state of mind.
